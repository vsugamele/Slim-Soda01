01 I forgot a word mid-sentence. Is this normal aging or early Alzheimer's? +

Short answer: Almost always normal age-related retrieval slowdown, not Alzheimer's disease.

After 50, "tip-of-the-tongue" moments become more common — you know the word, you just can't retrieve it as fast. This is a retrieval problem, not a storage problem, and it's a normal part of healthy aging.

True Alzheimer's involves persistent memory loss that disrupts daily life: forgetting recently-learned information, getting lost in familiar places, confusion with time or place. If you're experiencing those patterns, please see a neurologist. MemoPryl is a dietary supplement designed to support natural memory recall in healthy adults — it does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.

02 How is lithium orotate different from prescription lithium? +

Different molecule, different dose, different regulatory category.

Prescription lithium (lithium carbonate or lithium citrate) is dosed at 300–1,800 mg/day for mood disorders. It has a narrow therapeutic window and requires regular blood-level monitoring because the dose is close to the toxicity threshold.

Lithium orotate, the form in MemoPryl, is dosed at 5 mg/day — about 100× smaller. It is classified as a dietary supplement by the FDA (not a prescription drug), and is sold over the counter. The orotate carrier is thought to help the lithium cross cell membranes more efficiently at low doses.

Both compounds contain lithium, but they are not interchangeable. Do not stop prescription lithium and start MemoPryl — that is a decision for your prescribing doctor.

03 Is 5 mg really enough? It seems very small. +

Yes — the microdose is the whole point.

The 2025 Nature paper on lithium and memory described effects at doses 100× smaller than what pharmaceutical companies ever tested. The reason pharmaceutical companies never patented low-dose lithium is that you can't patent a dose of a naturally occurring mineral — there's no financial incentive.

A useful analogy: folate vs folic acid. Same compound, different dose, different use. The microdose works at the cellular level (mitochondrial function, BDNF support) without producing the blood-level spikes associated with prescription lithium.

We're not saying 5 mg is a magic number. We're saying the research summary supports it, and 90 days is enough time to notice a difference — or your money back.

07 Why is lithium orotate sold as a supplement and not a drug? +

Regulatory classification depends on dose and intended use, not just the molecule.

The FDA classifies substances as drugs when they are "intended for use in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease" or when their dose is "recognized by experts as requiring medical supervision."

Lithium orotate at 5 mg falls below the threshold where medical supervision is required, and is sold as a dietary supplement for general wellness and memory support. MemoPryl makes no drug claims — we use structure-function language ("may support memory and recall") because that's what the regulatory framework permits at this dose.

If a product claims lithium orotate "treats" or "prevents" Alzheimer's, that's a red flag — they're making drug claims without FDA approval. MemoPryl doesn't do that.
